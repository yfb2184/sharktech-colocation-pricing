## Why 100Gbps Connectivity Stopped Being Exotic

For roughly the last decade, 10Gbps uplinks were the unspoken industry standard in mid-tier data centers. Bundled 10G circuits did the heavy lifting, and most colocation customers never came close to saturating them. But the math has changed. Video traffic, AI inference workloads, large-scale game hosting, and always-on DDoS mitigation have pushed sustained bandwidth demands well past what bundled 10G can comfortably absorb.

A single 100G circuit eliminates the physical separation problems that come with bundling multiple 10G links. Even with the best load-balancing algorithms, bundled circuits still create congestion hotspots and jitter under burst conditions. Moving to native 100G uplinks gives a facility greater burst capacity on any given carrier and dramatically improves resilience against incoming DDoS attacks — a consideration that matters more than ever as attack sizes routinely cross the hundreds-of-gigabits threshold.

The interesting part: they absorbed the cost without passing it on to customers. So if you're hunting for colocation with 100Gbps connectivity, that upgrade is now baked into the plan pricing rather than tacked on as a premium surcharge.

## What to Actually Look for in a 100Gbps Colocation Provider

Before we get into specific plans, it's worth pinning down what "100Gbps connectivity" should actually mean when a provider claims it. Not every facility advertising 100Gbps is delivering the same thing.

**Redundancy, not just speed.** A single 100G uplink is a single point of failure. What you want is multi-100G redundant connectivity — multiple 100G circuits from different transit providers, ideally Tier 1 and Tier 3 carriers, so a fiber cut or a carrier dispute doesn't take your rack offline.

**DDoS absorption capacity.** 100Gbps connectivity is only as useful as your facility's ability to weather an attack. Look for providers that publish their mitigation capacity (Sharktech cites 300Gbps+ per location) and have a documented history in DDoS protection rather than reselling a third-party scrubbing service.

**On-site engineering, 24/7.** Remote hands are fine for rebooting a server. For colocation with serious bandwidth requirements, you want on-site engineers who can physically address carrier issues, swap optics, and troubleshoot at the patch panel at 3 a.m. on a Sunday.

**Geographic diversity.** If your audience is global, a single facility is a single point of failure. Providers with presence at major connection points in both the U.S. and EU give you failover options and lower latency to end users in different regions.

**Transparent rack pricing.** The colocation market has turned a corner recently — North American rates are up around 17% over the last five years after nearly 15 years of decline, with averages hovering around $98/kW/month. That makes transparent, published rack pricing genuinely valuable. Custom-quote-only providers often hide sticker shock until the contract stage.

## The 100Gbps Upgrade Story — And Why It Matters for You

Here's the part that's easy to overlook when you're comparing spec sheets. When a provider upgrades their core uplinks from 10G to 100G, the benefit isn't just "more bandwidth for the same price." It changes the physics of how the network behaves under load.

With bundled 10G circuits, even sophisticated load balancing can't eliminate congestion hotspots during traffic spikes or sustained attacks. Jitter creeps in. Latency gets inconsistent. Under a serious DDoS event, the attack traffic competes with legitimate traffic for the same physical circuits. Moving to native 100G uplinks collapses those bottlenecks. Burst capacity on any single carrier goes up by an order of magnitude, and the network's ability to absorb and scrub attack traffic improves correspondingly.
